MS. NOVA: 20 TEST QUESTIONS WITH EXPECTED ANSWERS
Use after uploading the test corpus.

01. [Machine Learning Fundamentals] What is data leakage, and why should preprocessing be fitted on the training set only?
Expected: Leakage gives the model information unavailable at prediction time; fit preprocessing using training data and apply it to other splits.

02. [Machine Learning Fundamentals] How do precision and recall differ?
Expected: Precision measures the proportion of predicted positives that are correct, while recall measures the proportion of actual positives detected.

03. [Neural Networks and Backpropagation] How is backpropagation different from an optimizer?
Expected: Backpropagation computes gradients using the chain rule; an optimizer uses gradients to update weights and biases.

04. [Neural Networks and Backpropagation] What problems are vanishing and exploding gradients?
Expected: Repeated derivatives shrink or grow through layers, weakening learning signals or causing unstable updates.

05. [Computer Vision and CNNs] What is the difference between object detection and instance segmentation?
Expected: Object detection returns class labels and bounding boxes; instance segmentation assigns separate pixel masks to individual objects.

06. [Computer Vision and CNNs] Why does parameter sharing make CNNs efficient?
Expected: Convolution filters reuse the same learned weights across many image positions.

07. [NLP and Embeddings] How do static and contextual word embeddings differ?
Expected: Static embeddings give a word type one vector; contextual representations depend on the surrounding text.

08. [NLP and Embeddings] Why is cosine similarity not proof of factual agreement?
Expected: Related or contradictory passages can share semantic topic and have similar embeddings.

09. [Transformers and LLMs] What are queries, keys and values in scaled dot-product attention?
Expected: Queries compare against keys to calculate attention weights, which are used to combine value vectors.

10. [Transformers and LLMs] How many 16x16 and 32x32 patches fit into a 224x224 image?
Expected: 196 patches at 16x16; 49 patches at 32x32.

11. [Reinforcement Learning] Why does Q-learning count as off-policy?
Expected: It targets the maximum next-state action value even when the behavior policy selected a different exploratory action.

12. [Reinforcement Learning] How does SARSA differ from Q-learning?
Expected: SARSA uses the next action actually taken by the behavior policy; Q-learning targets the best estimated next action.

13. [RAG and Hybrid Search] What different problems do BM25 and embedding retrieval solve?
Expected: BM25 finds lexical matches; dense retrieval finds semantic matches even when wording differs.

14. [RAG and Hybrid Search] Why use Reciprocal Rank Fusion before a cross-encoder reranker?
Expected: Rank fusion merges differently scored result lists using ranks; a cross-encoder then more carefully assesses query-passage pairs.

15. [Generative AI Models] How are GANs trained?
Expected: A generator creates samples while a discriminator learns to tell generated samples from real ones.

16. [Generative AI Models] How does a diffusion model generate an image?
Expected: It begins with noise and repeatedly applies learned denoising steps, optionally guided by text conditioning.

17. [Responsible AI] Why is an RL action propensity not a confidence-of-correctness score?
Expected: Propensity is the probability of selecting an action under a policy, not the probability that it is objectively correct.

18. [Responsible AI] Why must simulated results be labeled clearly?
Expected: Simulation depends on assumptions and does not establish measured real-world learning improvements.

19. [AI Agents] What tasks might Planner, Retriever, Teacher, and Critic perform?
Expected: Planning the lesson, finding source passages, drafting the response, and checking grounding and requirements.

20. [AI Agents] What should an agent do if uploaded notes contain no evidence for the requested explanation?
Expected: Acknowledge the missing evidence instead of inventing citations or falsely claiming support.
